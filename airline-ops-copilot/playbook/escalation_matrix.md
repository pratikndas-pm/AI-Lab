# Escalation Matrix

## Escalation Levels

### Level 0: Frontline Agent (No Escalation)
Handle directly:
- Minor delays (< 30 min)
- Standard rebooking (no special pax)
- Routine baggage issues
- General passenger inquiries

### Level 1: Duty Manager
Escalate for:
- Delays 30-120 minutes affecting > 50 passengers
- Special assistance passengers (wheelchair, UMNR, medical)
- Crew legality concerns
- Aircraft swap/equipment changes
- Passenger complaints requiring compensation
- Baggage issues with high-value items

### Level 2: OCC (Operations Control Center)
Escalate for:
- Delays > 120 minutes
- Flight cancellations
- Multiple flight impacts (cascade delays)
- Weather/ATC significant disruptions
- Crew shortage affecting operations
- Airport curfew risks
- Mass rebooking scenarios (> 100 pax)

### Level 3: Senior Management
Escalate for:
- Safety incidents
- Security threats
- VIP/media incidents
- Major operational meltdown (> 5 flights impacted)
- Regulatory violations
- Significant financial exposure (> $100K)
- Reputation risk (viral social media, press)

## Special Passenger Escalation (ALWAYS)
- **UMNR (Unaccompanied Minor)**: Level 1 minimum
- **Wheelchair/Medical**: Level 1 if delay > 1 hour
- **VIP/High-tier frequent flyer**: Level 1 if complaint
- **Group travel > 20 pax**: Level 1 for rebooking
- **Pregnant passengers (late term)**: Level 1 if delay causes concern
- **Pet in cargo**: Level 1 if delay > 2 hours or temperature concern

## Time-Based Auto-Escalation
- **60 minutes**: Alert Duty Manager
- **120 minutes**: Notify OCC
- **180+ minutes or cancellation**: OCC takes control

## How to Escalate
1. Gather all relevant info (flight, pax count, cause, current status)
2. Document actions taken so far
3. State specific decision needed or assistance required
4. Contact via: Radio > Phone > Email (in order of urgency)
5. Log escalation in system

## DO NOT Escalate:
- Routine passenger requests (seat changes, meals)
- Issues you have authority to resolve
- To avoid making a decision you're authorized to make
- Without gathering basic information first

## Red Flags (Immediate Escalation)
- Passenger medical emergency
- Unruly/intoxicated passenger
- Security concern
- Crew reporting safety issue
- Child traveling alone missed connection
- Conflict with local authorities
- Media/press on scene

## Escalation Response Time
- **Level 1**: 5-10 minutes
- **Level 2**: 10-20 minutes
- **Level 3**: 30-60 minutes

## Documentation
Every escalation must include:
- Timestamp
- Who escalated
- Why escalated
- To whom
- Decision/outcome
- Follow-up actions
